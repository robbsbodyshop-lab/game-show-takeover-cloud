# Wheel of Fortune: the rules this game follows

This is the reference the Wheel of Fortune game is built and tested against.
It follows the current syndicated show (2024-present) as closely as a party
game played by teams can. It was researched from episode recaps, the show's
fan wikis, Wikipedia and the licensed live-tour rules (there is no published
official rulebook). Where the show is ambiguous or a party game needs a
different answer, the choice made here is marked **(app choice)**.

Teams stand in for the three contestants and take turns in play order (the
door-selection order when one was drawn, otherwise team order). "Clockwise"
means the next team in that order.

## Episode structure

1. **Opening toss-ups** (host setting, classic by default):
   - _Classic:_ a $1,000 Toss-Up, then a $2,000 Toss-Up whose winner starts
     Round 1.
   - _Fortune Frenzy (Season 44):_ 30 seconds of back-to-back $1,000
     toss-up puzzles in one category; whoever solves the last one starts
     Round 1.
2. **Round 1**: top dollar $2,500. The Wild Card, the Million Dollar Wedge and
   a trip wedge are on the wheel.
3. **Round 2, the Mystery Round**: top dollar $3,500, two Mystery wedges.
4. **Round 3, the Prize Puzzle**: top dollar $3,500, the Express wedge; the
   solver also wins the Prize Puzzle trip.
5. **Triple Toss-Up**: three toss-ups in one category and theme, $2,000 each,
   plus $4,000 for sweeping all three. The third solver starts Round 4.
6. **Round 4**: top dollar $5,000, no special wedges. The host rings the time
   bell to turn it into the **Speed-Up** (a host setting can start Round 4 as
   the Speed-Up); every game ends in a Speed-Up. If Round 4 is solved before
   the bell, one more $5,000 round opens as the Speed-Up **(app choice)**.
7. **Bonus Round** for the team with the highest total (a host setting can
   leave it out).

The short format (host setting) plays one $2,000 toss-up (or Fortune Frenzy),
Rounds 1 and 2, then a $5,000 round that opens as the Speed-Up, then the Bonus
Round **(app choice)**. The host can ring the bell early in any round to finish
sooner.

Round 2 starts with the team after the Round 1 starter, Round 3 with the team
after that. If a toss-up that decides a starter goes unsolved, the first team
in play order starts **(show: the player nearest the host)**.

## Toss-ups

Letters appear one at a time at random. Once the first letter shows, any team
may buzz in and must answer at once (a buzz before it does nothing). A
correct answer banks the toss-up value immediately (Bankrupt can never take
it, and it cannot buy vowels). A wrong answer locks that team out of that
puzzle only; the reveal continues. If the puzzle fills in, nobody wins
it. In Fortune Frenzy a wrong answer throws the puzzle out and the next one
starts, and the 30-second clock keeps running while a team answers.

## A turn

- **Spin**, then call a consonant (Y is a consonant). Every occurrence is
  revealed and pays the wedge value times the count into the round bank.
- **Buy a vowel** for a flat $250 from this round's spendable cash (at the
  start of a turn or after a correct letter). Vowels earn nothing.
- **Solve** at the start of a turn or after a correct letter. A team that
  spins and solves without calling a letter is accepted if right but earns
  nothing for that spin.
- The turn passes on: a consonant not in the puzzle, any letter already
  called, a vowel not in the puzzle (the $250 is still spent), a wrong solve,
  Lose a Turn, Bankrupt, or running out of time **(timer is a host setting:
  off, 5 or 10 seconds; the host can change it mid-game)**. A team whose
  answer began before its clock ran out may be given its solve back, until
  the team that got the turn acts **(app choice)**.
  A vowel called right after a spin is ignored and the team is asked for a
  consonant.
- When every vowel is revealed, vowels can no longer be bought. When every
  consonant is revealed, nobody may spin: teams buy vowels or solve. If every
  team then fails in turn and nobody can buy a vowel, the puzzle is thrown
  out unsolved **(app choice)**; a turn lost to a Mystery flip's Bankrupt
  does not count toward that, so the flipping team still gets its try.
- A fully revealed puzzle must still be solved aloud by the team in control.

## Solving

The answer must match the puzzle word for word: nothing added or dropped (a
missing plural S is wrong). Punctuation, spacing and case do not matter, and a
lead-in like "It's a..." is accepted. The host can overrule the app either
way. When the host reverses a right ruling, every team has already seen the
answer, so a puzzle still in play is thrown out and a replacement comes up;
the turn and the money stay as the reversal leaves them (the turn passes on
as after any wrong solve) **(app choice)**.

Only the solver keeps the round: their round cash plus round prizes are
banked, raised to **$1,000** if lower. Everyone else's round money and prizes
are erased. Banked money is never at risk.

## The wheel

24 wedges: cash from $500 to $900, one top-dollar wedge, two Bankrupts and one
Lose a Turn, laid out as in the show's current wheel as far as it could be
reconstructed.

- **Bankrupt**: round cash to $0, round prizes lost, and any Wild Card or
  Million Dollar Wedge the team holds is lost. Banked money is safe.
- **Lose a Turn**: the turn passes; nothing is lost.
- **Wild Card**: the wedge is on the wheel in Rounds 1-3 until a team claims
  it with a correct consonant ($500 each). The card is then usable in any
  round, Round 4 included, until the Speed-Up: once, after a correct
  consonant on a dollar value, the holder may play it to call one more
  consonant at the same value (not while riding the Express). Unused, it
  gives a fourth consonant in the Bonus Round. Kept even without solving;
  lost to Bankrupt; not usable in the Speed-Up.
- **Million Dollar Wedge** (Rounds 1-3): a ONE MILLION third between two
  Bankrupt thirds. Claim it with a correct consonant ($0 per letter); keep it
  by solving that round and never hitting Bankrupt; it turns the $100,000
  Bonus Wheel envelope into $1,000,000.
- **Trip wedge** (Round 1, carried into Round 2 if unclaimed): $500 per letter
  plus the trip, kept only by solving the round.
- **Mystery wedges** (Round 2): $1,000 per letter. After a correct letter the
  team keeps the money or gives it up to flip the wedge: one hides $10,000 (a
  prize, not spendable), the other Bankrupt. After one flip, the other is a
  plain $1,000.
- **Express** (Round 3): $1,000 per letter, then PLAY or PASS. Playing, the
  team calls consonants at $1,000 each without spinning and may buy vowels
  and solve; any miss, repeat or wrong solve is a Bankrupt.
- **Same Letter** puzzles pay a flat $1,000 bonus for calling the shared
  letter.

Specials lost to Bankrupt or an unsolved round do not go back on the wheel.

## The Speed-Up

The team in control spins once (non-cash results are re-spun). Every consonant
for the rest of the round is worth that value plus $1,000; vowels are free and
worth nothing. Teams take one action each in turn: call one letter or solve.
After a correct letter the team has 3 seconds to solve. A miss, a repeat, a
wrong solve or running out of time passes play. The solver banks the round
(house minimum applies) and the game ends.

## The Bonus Round

The team with the highest total (cash plus prize values) plays; a tie is
broken by a toss-up between the tied teams worth $1,000. A team that won
nothing gets $1,000.

1. The team picks one of three categories.
2. It spins the Bonus Wheel: 24 envelopes from $40,000 to $100,000 (six of
   them a car worth $40,000), with the $100,000 envelope worth $1,000,000 if
   the team holds the Million Dollar Wedge. The host keeps the envelope
   sealed.
3. R, S, T, L, N and E are revealed. The team calls three more consonants and
   one vowel (four consonants with a Wild Card); repeats and invalid calls are
   simply asked again.
4. Ten seconds to solve, any number of guesses. A correct answer before the
   clock starts also wins.
5. The envelope is opened win or lose.

## How it maps to the event

Every team keeps the money it banked, as on the show. On the event scoreboard
**$100 of Wheel money is 1 point** **(app choice, so Wheel and Feud scores are
on the same scale)**, rounded, for every team. The points come from each
team's main-game total: banked cash plus the value of the prizes won in the
main game (trips, the Mystery $10,000, the Prize Puzzle trip), with the
tiebreaker and consolation $1,000 included. The Bonus Round prize is **not**
added **(app choice: a $40,000 to $1,000,000 envelope would outweigh every
other game of the event)**; winning the Bonus Round earns a bonus Plinko chip
instead. The game winner gets a Plinko chip.
